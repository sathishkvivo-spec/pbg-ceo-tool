Section 02

The Framework

Past reports showed Vivo present in store. That was not enough.

Presence is being on the shelf. Dominance is being chosen.

This framework measures dominance — not presence. These are not the same thing.

Every upgrade decision traces back to a score. No opinion. No assumption.

Presence ≠ Dominance

D1

Vivo Brand Strength

Max 40

Measures every Vivo branding asset present in the store.

D2

Competitive Pressure Index

Max 30

Measures competitor branding pressure in the same store.

D3

Store Premium Potential

Max 30

Measures whether the store can drive premium Vivo sellout.

3-question test — remove any one dimension and the decision breaks

Dimension Question It Answers Why It Cannot Be Removed
D1 — Vivo Brand Strength Where are we today? Without this, we don't know our current state
D2 — Competitive Pressure Index Who are we fighting and how hard? Without this, our score has no competitive context
D3 — Store Premium Potential Is this store worth fighting for? Without this, upgrade decisions have no ROI logic

How Apple and Samsung Engineer Retail Dominance

Apple

  • Mandatory Experience Zone at entry — first brand every customer sees
  • Minimum 6 live handsets on dedicated Apple-only table
  • Proprietary Apple furniture — retailer cannot modify
  • Contractual first-right-of-refusal on entry wall space
  • Apple-certified staff, trained and paid by Apple
  • Premium lighting at Apple's cost

Samsung

  • Space rental paid as percentage of store sellout — not fixed
  • Retailer earns more as Samsung sells more — financial alignment
  • Dedicated Samsung Experience Zone, branded furniture mandatory
  • Co-branded presence on Main Signage Board in MT stores
  • No competitor products within Samsung exclusion zone
  • Regular Samsung merchandising team visits

This is why D2 exists — to measure competitive reality honestly, not just Vivo's presence.

Reality A — Negotiable Space

Standard upgrade prescription. Install experience table, backlit panel, counter branding immediately.

Reality B — Negotiable Displacement

Competitor asset present but retailer open to discussion. ROI argument: Vivo volume movement > competitor value model.

Reality C — Financially Locked

Samsung and Apple pay retailer space rental as a percentage of their store sellout — not a fixed fee. This creates financial alignment: the retailer earns more as those brands sell more. Vivo operates on a fixed rental model. Direct space competition in financially locked stores is not viable. Strategy: position quality over quantity, OOH compensation, and a long-term commercial argument.
